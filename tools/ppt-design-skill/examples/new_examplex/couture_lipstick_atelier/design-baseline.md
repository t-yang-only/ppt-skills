# COUTURE COLOR — Design Baseline

This case is the benchmark for luxury beauty/editorial presentation work in
this repository. It is a visual and structural reference, not a template to
copy literally.

## What the benchmark proves

- A product story can read as a fashion editorial rather than a cosmetics
  catalogue.
- A small palette can create luxury when material roles are explicit: oxblood
  for object and emphasis, bone for relief, champagne for precision, and rose
  for softness.
- Photography carries face, hand, skin, and reflective material; native
  PowerPoint objects carry the product system, shade logic, annotations, and
  sequence.
- A deck can alternate cinematic pages with bone-paper study pages without
  changing its identity.
- Whitespace is assigned a job: let the face breathe, isolate the object, or
  give the proposition a pause.

## Transfer rules

1. Start with one image-led anchor and one sentence-level point of view.
2. Keep one display serif, one utility face, and one body face throughout.
3. Use fine rules and small labels to establish precision; do not add random
   decoration.
4. Make at least one product explanation, one color system, and one sequence
   fully editable.
5. Reuse a subject or gesture only when the narrative advances; never repeat a
   photo as filler.
6. When a page is sparse, the focal object must become larger or more legible,
   not be replaced by extra copy.

## Page-role map

| Pages | Role | Benchmark evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 01, 08 | cinematic image pages | image-first read, controlled dark field |
| 02, 09 | narrative structure | proposition and release rhythm |
| 03, 07 | editable systems | swatches, silhouettes, tonal families |
| 04 | product grammar | native object geometry and callouts |
| 05 | material evidence | macro image plus material index |
| 06, 10 | point of view | one gesture, one final statement |

## Do not copy blindly

This benchmark is successful because its page architecture follows the story.
If the next case has a different audience or domain, preserve the discipline
of focal point, material hierarchy, editable structure, and evidence-based QA,
not the lipstick motif or exact page sequence.
